## Title

**DHAGO — Time, Woven.**

## Short description

DHAGO is a custom Video.js v10 skin inspired by *dhago*—the Nepali word for thread. Real chapter cues become a tactile woven timeline, with a shuttle-like seek thumb following actual playback while play, time, sound, captions, speed and fullscreen remain immediately familiar. Linen, Ink and Paper give the same interface three distinct atmospheres from narrow mobile layouts to widescreen viewing.

## Design thinking

What if video time were something you could weave?

DHAGO turns chapter intervals into pieces of cloth joined by a visible thread that moves forward—and backward—with the film. A textured shuttle, cue-derived chapter ribbon and solid control shelf carry the textile idea into the controls themselves without sacrificing the clock, captions, keyboard focus or familiar playback behaviour.

The inspiration is intentionally broad: the design draws from the idea of thread and woven structure in Nepali textile culture without claiming to reproduce or authenticate a named traditional motif.

The original 64-second *Studies in Thread* motion study was made specifically for the skin. Its four scenes—light, quiet, shadow and return—give the chapter ribbon a calm visual rhythm and let the interface remain the focus.

## What judges should notice first

1. **The skin is the visual idea.** The woven chapter ribbon, shuttle thumb, control shelf and typography carry the identity without relying on decorative page chrome.
2. **Seeking remains familiar.** The textile metaphor changes the material language of the timeline, not the mental model of scrubbing.
3. **The details survive interaction.** Pause, reverse seeking, replay, captions, speed, sound, fullscreen and chapter changes all remain visually coherent.
4. **The design adapts.** Linen, Ink and Paper change atmosphere without changing the interaction model.
5. **Narrow layouts remain deliberate.** Controls stay bounded and usable at mobile widths and in the dedicated 240px edge study.

## Why DHAGO stands out

### A memorable concept that lives inside the controls

The woven idea is not a decorative border around a standard player. Chapter geometry, the seek thumb, progress thread, chapter readout and finished state all participate in the same concept.

### Familiarity under the surface

Every expressive choice is paired with a conventional playback affordance: a clear seek thumb, readable clock, recognizable play and sound controls, named settings, visible focus and a stable caption area.

### Honest motion

The interface follows the media itself. Seeking backward unwinds the visual state; pausing stops it; replay begins again. Buffering and missing information are not disguised as progress.

### Accessibility as visual polish

Essential controls use comfortable touch targets, keyboard focus remains visible, the control shelf isolates text and icons from changing footage, captions are constrained to a readable area, and reduced-motion and forced-colour environments have explicit treatments.

### Craft across states

The same visual system is resolved for poster, active playback, captions, menus, chapter transitions, narrow layouts, missing chapters, audio-only media, errors, completion and replay. The result is intended to feel finished rather than merely themed.

### Reusable without becoming generic

The competition skin is not coupled to the showcase page. The same mount is used by the main collection and Studio Journal, and its appearance can shift between Linen, Ink and Paper without changing the interaction model.

The woven timeline is also separable from the full skin. It accepts chapter geometry and real playback state, returns normalized progress and active-chapter state, and can drive seeking in another host treatment without depending on the DHAGO page itself.

The Edge States Lab goes one step further: another compatible player can be connected through a small browser-side bridge loaded from a CORS-readable module URL or a local JavaScript file. External code runs inside an opaque-origin sandboxed frame, local files stay in the browser rather than being uploaded, and loading failures are kept separate from actual skin failures. This makes the resilience work reusable evidence rather than a one-off private test page.

## Originality and attribution

*Studies in Thread* is an original four-part procedural motion study with synthesized ambient audio, no spoken narration and English descriptive captions. The illustrated poster is original artwork rather than a film frame.

DHAGO's textile language is inspired by the meaning of *dhago* and woven structure in general. It does not claim that its vectors reproduce a named traditional Nepali pattern.

## Compatibility boundary

Verified automated browser evidence covers Chrome, Firefox and a WebKit engine, including playback, seeking, chapters, captions and responsive layouts within the tested scope. WebKit automation is not a claim of physical Safari testing.

Physical iPhone Safari, physical Android Chrome, macOS Safari and real VoiceOver/NVDA sessions remain **not tested** and must not be represented otherwise.
