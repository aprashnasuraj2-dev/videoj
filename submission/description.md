DHAGO turns video time into a woven ribbon. Chapters become distinct woven sections, while real playback conditions can leave an honest viewing history in the cloth: active rendition or decoded-resolution changes alter thread thickness, actual waiting/stalled intervals leave slack, and exposed dropped-frame counters leave missed stitches. Drawing from Nepali woven material language, the interface keeps standard Video.js v10 controls familiar across Linen, Ink and Paper treatments while adding accessible chapter navigation and layered metadata weft.

The project also publishes two reusable proofs alongside the player: a host-neutral Woven Time Ribbon and a browser-side Stress Lab that lets other compatible player authors load an ESM or local adapter, run constrained-container and hostile-metadata fixtures, and export structured results. The original 64-second motion study is delivered through Mux with a local compatibility path. Claims remain bounded: decoded dimensions are not bitrate, fixture speaker/sponsor data is labeled as fixture data, and Safari, physical phones, VoiceOver and NVDA remain NOT TESTED unless genuinely tested before submission.
